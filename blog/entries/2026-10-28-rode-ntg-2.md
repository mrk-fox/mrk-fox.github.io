# Trying and failing to fix the Røde NTG 2

A few months ago a fellow guy gifted me a broken Røde NTG 2. This piece of audioelectronics resembles a decent shotgun mic with a possibility for use without a 48V phantom power source by inserting an AA battery into the mic. The primary cause of failure was a broken XLR port that seemed to be ripped out by pulling too hard on the XLR cable. That didn't seem like an issue to me and so began the magic journey of ✨repair✨. I stripped the 3 cables going to the port off the PCB and soldered new ones connecting these two. Seemes finished, right? Right?

![Padme meme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrk-fox/mrk-fox.github.io/refs/heads/main/blog/entries/assets/padme_ntg.jpeg)

Well, silly me was a bit too rough on the delicate cables connecting the microphone capsule itself to the PCB ripping one off. Trying to solder the one ripped cable back to the PCB I ripped off another. With 2 out of 3 cables voting for separation from the PCB, I followed the advice cutting off and then soldering the mic back to the place it belongs. 

![Broken Mic Cables](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrk-fox/mrk-fox.github.io/refs/heads/main/blog/entries/assets/ntg5.jpeg)

Having that done and the mic assembled, it should now work! I am highly stressing the word "should" because after connecting it with and without 48V phantom power it didn't work. I even tested whether the mixer was broken by using a working mic but this didn't help the fact that the microphone didn't work. Luckily, the RØDE engineers never had in mind somebody aiming to disassemble their precious product, so they didn't bother hiding the testing pads on the PCB. 
Deviating a bit off the topic but I must give props to the developers of this device as they didn't bother to leave out GLUE for assembly (looking at you, Apple).

Anyway, after I now ragebaited myself into wanting that mic fixed I decided to look at the circuitry and understand the cause of defect. The amplifier itself seemed to be alright. I used my ears as an oscillatior and a needle grounded to my hand as a noise generator. So, my deduction leads to the suspicion that the capsule itself is broken. 

At this point the "simple" soldering repair converged into a full scale reverse engineering and electrical engineering task which was, due to the lack of an oscilloscope and signal generator, out of my scope.
